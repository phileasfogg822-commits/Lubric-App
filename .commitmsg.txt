Party-wise price memory in the billing screen

"What did I last charge THIS customer for THIS product, and for how many
litres" - answered under the product as you add it, instead of opening three
bills to dig for it. Zafar Iqbal's Castrol GTX has been billed 4,700, then
3,000, then 4,000 across three bills, which nobody can hold in their head.

- A 0.68rem micro-row under each cart line's qty x price, up to two past
  sales: "last 3.5L . Rs 3,500 . 2026-09-30". Clicking one applies that price
  to the line. Pro-rated lines are prefixed ~, because with bill-by-capacity
  the stored unit_price is a fraction of the bottle and offering it as if it
  were the bottle price would be quietly wrong.
- Litres are shown because 3.5L and a whole 4L are different sales at
  different prices. Typing 3.5L into the Capacity box narrows the memory to
  what was sold at 3.5L to this customer - that is the price that would apply.
  With the box empty nothing is filtered, and each entry shows its own litres.
  A past sale that recorded no capacity falls back to the pack size.
- The right-hand panel gains a "Prices you last sold" list, so the last agreed
  price is visible when the customer walks in, and clicking one adds the
  product to the bill at that price.
- "Make returning customer" for a walking customer who has been billed
  WALKING_PROMOTION_THRESHOLD (3) or more times, plus the bill count in the
  panel header. Never automatic: pf() picks price_walking for walking and
  price_returning for returning, so promoting changes which list price applies
  from then on. That is the intent, and also why it is a button.

Strictly per customer, as asked: a walking customer with no customer row has no
memory, and a product's list price is never offered as a stand-in - a price
someone else paid is a different deal.

- src/lib/priceMemory.ts is deliberately pure (no db, no React), so the rules
  are unit-tested with no browser: server/unit_price_memory.js, 18 checks.
  One of them caught a real flaw - the function trusted the caller to pass rows
  newest-first and silently returned the OLDEST prices labelled "last" when
  they weren't. It now sorts defensively.
- server/e2e_price_memory.js runs the real query against the shop, 7 checks:
  a voided bill is excluded even though voidOrder KEEPS the row as
  'cancelled', service lines never appear as product prices, one customer
  never sees another's prices, and the index exists.
- No DISTINCT ON and no = ANY anywhere: both are Postgres-only and
  pgToSqlite translates neither, so either would have worked in the browser
  and broken the offline desktop.
- Migration 023 indexes order_items(kind, ref_id, order_id) and
  orders(customer_id, created_at DESC). The only index on order_items was
  order_id, so this read scanned every line ever sold.

Also fixes a latent offline bug found on the way: the sync agent inserts every
server column of order_items except id, but the local SQLite table was missing
uuid, shop_id, updated_at, capacity, leftover_litres, leftover_to,
leftover_released and billed_by_capacity. The pull would have thrown "no such
column" and taken the whole download down. Now in the local schema and in
SYNC_COLUMN_PATCHES, with the unique index on uuid the upsert needs.

This is the spec's Party-Wise Price Memory, so it lands as the first piece of
the white-label roadmap rather than a one-off.
